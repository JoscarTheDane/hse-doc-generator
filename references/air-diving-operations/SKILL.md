### Chapter 5 — Manning Levels & Working Practices
**Working hours**: max 12 hours continuous, 12 hours unbroken rest. Longest period 24 hours before 8 hours rest. Diving Supervisor has authority to prevent fatigued personnel from diving.

**Minimum dive team**: 1 IMCA Diving Supervisor, 2 Divers, 2 Diver Medics, 1 Dive Technician. One member per shift (other than diving — other than supervisor) trained as Diver Medic.

**Surface Compression Chambers**: operated by competent persons under Diving Supervisor supervision.

**Overlapping functions**: permitted if qualified and safe.

**Minimum supervisory**: 1 IMCA qualified SS diving supervisor per 12-hour shift. Additional DS required for back-to-back diving with two DDCs, in-water turnaround, or surface decompression during shift.

### Chapter 6 — Surface Supplied Diving Procedures
**Standards**: per IMCA D018, IMCA DESIGN D023/D040.

**SCUBA**: NOT AUTHORIZED on any worksite.

**Depth limit**: **NO air diving deeper than 50 msw**. SRP max 30 msw (exceptional up to 50 msw with risk assessment). All SRP diving to be **PLANNED no-decompression**.

**Live boating**: No except from Class 2 or 3 DP DSV.

**Oxygen limits**: Max PPO2 **1.4 bar** in any surface supplied diving. Min O2 content **200mb PPO2**.

**Standby Diver**: Primary duty = render assistance in emergency. Must be fully qualified diver with identical responsibilities. **Minimum 20 commercial dives**. Must have Repetitive Group Designator A. Fully dressed and ready to enter water immediately.

**Flying after diving**: Pain in limb only → analgesics/fluids/oxygen, continue flight. Any other symptoms → seek medical specialist advice, give 100% oxygen, reduce cabin altitude/divert. Emergency flight under 300m.

**Air supply**: Primary AND secondary gas supply to each diver. Standby diver's main from independent air supply. All supplies analysed for O2 downstream of last diver's supply valve. Primary and secondary from separate tested/analysed sources.

**Thermal protection**: wet suits + lycra + coveralls over top. Dry suits/hot water suits in colder conditions.

**Decompression chamber**: **Two compartment (twin lock) DDC** required. **60" diameter minimum**. ERP must include transport to nearest DDC/hospital. After decompression dives: remain within **20 min of suitable chamber for 4 hours**, then within **2 hours until 18 hours post-surfacing**. DDC ready for **18-hour bend watch** after last dive.

**Air diving operational procedures**: Bottom time = surface to start of final ascent. Use next deeper/longer table increment. Stop depth = pneumo at **chest level**. **No repetitive diving** — minimum 18 hours between hyperbaric exposures (emergency exception: combine bottom times if interval < 18h, use deepest depth).

**Pre/post dive checks**: Equipment inspected before each dive, rechecked **every 6 hours** during continuous operations. Serviceable = signed tag with date/maintenance. Unserviceable = red tag with fault. Checklists must be in tables for sign-off.

6-Hourly checks: control van, dive stages/baskets, A-frame/tuggers, oxygen quads, air quads, compressors, helmets, DDC.

Pre-dive: control van, deck/chamber, divers "call out" (each diver and standby).

Post-dive: supervisor's control van, DDC checks, equipment checks.

### Chapter 7 — Safety & Operational Provision
**Standards**: per IMCA D014/018/DESIGN D023/D040/D064. More stringent standard applies.

**Breathing apparatus**: All items labelled with tag, certificates, maintenance records signed by qualified technician. Must be checked before each use. Match to planned work (dry helmet for HP jetting, band mask + head protection for standby). **All bail-out cylinders and pillar valves checked with calibrated Go/No-Go gauges** — failing items marked "DO NOT USE".

**Bail-out**: Minimum **1 minute per 10 metres of umbilical from nearest safe haven**. **Breathing rate: 50 LPM** (40 LPM IMCA minimum + 10 LPM safety factor). Calculation: volume available = (charged pressure - 20 bar) × 1000 ÷ (50 LPM × absolute pressure at depth).

**Chamber operation**: Two independent means of pressurisation. Sufficient compressed air for both chamber locks to maximum treatment depth + **three complete surface decompression cycles** (IMCA D050). **90 m³ oxygen** minimum for emergency treatment. **Operational diving ceases when oxygen < 90 m³ usable**. Chamber temperature: optimum **24°C (76°F)**. **No oil/grease/hydrocarbons** in chambers — only silicon-based lubricants. **Chamber oxygen not to exceed 23%** at any time.

**Communications**: Voice comms between Diver and Diving Supervisor must be recorded, kept **48 hours minimum**. Terminate dive if comms fail until restored. **Diving Supervisor must always hear diver's breathing** even if others joined link. **Diving Supervisor must be able to disconnect all other personnel immediately**. Dedicated hard wire links to bridge, ROV control (SIMOPS), crane.

**Line signals**: Royal Navy protocol. Pull = long steady tension. Bells = short tugs in pairs. All signals preceded by one pull to attract attention. Only when all comms have failed.

**Umbilical marking**: All umbilicals marked at **5 metre intervals** with coloured tape. Chart available in dive control and at tender. Safe length calculated per hazard/bailout duration/tender position.

**Weather/environmental constraints**: Wind 10kts, Current 1kt (normal), 1-1.2kt with risk assessment + monitoring. Swell: 0.5-1m standard air decompression, 1-2m any in-water decompression. Visibility: 500m (general), 200m (platform/moored). Fog: under side working within 500m zone stopped.

**DP DSV**: Alarms at dive control, bridge DP desk, main deck, Supt cabin, Master cabin, ECR. Dedicated primary/secondary hard wire to bridge. DP alerts:
- **Green**: Full operations
- **Yellow**: Suspend operations, make safe, move to safe location ("golden gate")
- **Red**: Return immediately to deployment device and surface
- **DCR always ready for immediate use during ALL air diving from DP DSV**

**Lifting**: Per IOGP/IMCA. Full lift plans and risk assessments. Only DS may issue instructions to crane driver. All equipment certified.

**Simultaneous ROV/diving**: Combined when same vessel, same structure, or within combined umbilical length. Dual responsibility of Diving Supt/Sup and ROV Supervisor. Umbilical entanglement procedure: ROV pilot MUST NOT attempt to recover entangled vehicle — stabilise, inform Diving Supervisor, diver frees entanglement.

**HP jetting**: IMCA D049. Trigger MUST NOT be locked in "open" position. Two-handed gun control. Pump shut down during all diver movements. Retro jet mandatory on all guns. DP acoustic interference — inform DP operators.

**Cutting/Welding**: Oxy-arc: last resort only. Amperage controlled by double-pole single-throw knife switch (never manual). No diver as part of earth return circuit. Welding: insulated power source, earthed machine frame, no metallic diving equipment contacting electrode.

**Electricity underwater**: DC preferred. RCD and isolation transformers with insulation monitors.

**Inflatable craft**: Weather-dependent. Minimum equipment list (radio, first aid, pyrotechnics, paddles, flares, etc.). Minimum crew of two. Coxswain must be appointed by Senior Diving Supervisor with Certificate of Competence.

**Epoxy**: Toxic vapours. As little as **50g = significant inhalation hazard**. Barrier cream, disposable coveralls, separate downline. Decontamination: no solvent admitted to chamber while under pressure.

**Personnel basket**: Max 4 persons. Tested every 6 months (twice MWT). Daylight only. Lifejackets mandatory. Rescue boat ready.

**Hydraulic tools**: Dead man switches mandatory. No tying back or modification.

**Dredging/Seabed**: Air lifting and subsea dredging methods. Monitor spoil fall, inform DSV bridge of expanding air bubbles (interferes with DP reference systems).

**Mattress installation**: 3m above seabed when positioning. **Frequent cause of diver hand injuries** — briefing essential.

**Lift bags**: **NOT for surface load testing**. Hold backs mandatory. Inverter lines. Boyle's Law awareness — caution in 0-30m.

**Explosives**: Specialist task only, not in scope of this manual.

### Chapter 10 — Decompression Procedures & Tables
**US Navy Diving Manual Rev 7a tables used.**

**Key definitions**: Descent time, bottom time, total decompression time, total dive time, maximum depth, stage depth, surface interval, residual nitrogen, single dive, repetitive dive.

**Pneumo correction**: +1 fsw for 0-100 fsw, +2 fsw for 101-200 fsw.

**Three modes in Table 9-9**: In-water on air, in-water on air/O2, surface decompression on O2.

**In-water decompression on air**: Enter at next deeper depth and next longer bottom time. Read across "Air" row. Last stop at 20 fsw.

**In-water decompression on air/O2**: Follow air schedule to 30 fsw (or 20 fsw), shift to 100% O2. **Oxygen stops in bold**. **Air breaks: 5 min every 30 min on O2** (not required if total O2 time ≤ 35 min).

**Surface decompression on O2**: Complete in-water air stops at 40 fsw and deeper, surface at 40 fsw/min, **max surface interval 5 min** (3.5 min for undressing). Compress on air to 50 fsw (max 100 fsw/min). O2 at 50 fsw for 15 min, then 40 fsw for remainder. 5-min air break every 30 min O2.

**Bottom time limits**: Max 165 fsw / 50 msw. Max **4 hours in-water per 24 hours**. Back-to-back requires two chambers.

**Ascent rate**: **30 fsw/min** (20 sec per 10 fsw) between stops. 40 fsw/min from 40 fsw to surface for surface decompression. If > 40 fsw/min: STOP, wait, continue.

**Delays**: < 1 min = disregard. > 1 min at depth < 50 fsw = add to first stop. > 1 min at depth > 50 fsw = add to bottom time, recompute.

**Emergency procedures**:
- Bottom time exceeds table: read to deeper depth with longer schedule
- Surface interval > 5 min: 5-7 min → +15 min at 50 fsw. > 7 min → compress to 60 fsw, Table 5 or 6.
- DCI during surface interval: Type I resolves → +15 min at 50 fsw. Type I doesn't resolve or > 5 min → compress to 60 fsw. Type II → Table 6.
- Loss of O2 in chamber: 50/50 preferred (multiply remaining O2 time by 2). Air: multiply by air/O2 ratio, allocate 10%/20%/70% to 40/30/20 fsw stops.
- CNS oxygen toxicity: remove mask, wait 15 min after symptoms subside, resume. If convulsion: descend 10 fsw at 1 fsw/min, resume.
- Omitted decompression: < 1 min surface → return to stop, +1 min. 1-5 min → DDC, surface decompression. 5-7 min → DDC, +15 min at 50 fsw. > 7 min → DDC, Table 5 or 6. Deeper than 30 fsw stop omitted → Table 6.
- Uncontrolled ascent within no-decompression limits: observe for 1 hour.

**No-decompression limits**: Per Table 9-7. At ≤ 20 fsw: unlimited. At 60 fsw: 60 min max.

**Repetitive diving**: Policy — only in emergency, max 2 days. First dive max to group "O". Diver must not dive again until 12-hour surface period. Use Table 9-8 for residual nitrogen.

**Riser tables**: NOT Atlantic Marine policy unless project-specific RA and approval.

### Chapter 11 — Medical Emergencies Not Requiring Recompression
**General**: In all serious cases, seek medical assistance. Any post-dive illness assumed dive-related. Standby diver deployed at first indication of stress. Loss of consciousness underwater may be fatal.

**Squeeze**: Middle ear (stop descent, attempt equalise, abort if rupture), external ear (avoid tight headgear), sinus (stop descent, ascend slowly, decongestant drops), thoracic/lung (bring to surface, alert for recompression, drainage position), body (most serious/potentially fatal — bring to surface, alert for recompress).

**Respiratory**:
- O2 toxicity respiratory: UPTD = T × (2P-1)^0.833
- O2 toxicity CNS: **VENTID** (Vision, Ears, Nausea, Twitching, Irritability, Dizziness). Action: halt ascent, remove mask, protect from injury during convulsion. Wait 15 min (30 min for Table 7).
- Hypoxia: often no warning. Cyanosis of lips/fingernails, unconsciousness. Action: abort dive, switch to secondary supply.
- CO2 excess: headache, dizziness, confusion. Action: stop working, switch supply, ventilate.
- CO poisoning: **cherry-redness of lips/fingernails**. Action: abort dive, surfacing conscious → Table 5. Unconscious → **recompress to 165' and treat for gas embolism**.
- Toxic vapour inhalation, narcosis, broncho-spasm, pulmonary oedema, asphyxiation

**Other**:
- Hypothermia: re-warming in shower (38-44°C on trunk only, extremities outside), wrap in blankets, watch for re-warming shock, hot beverages.
- Near drowning: **cold-immersed victim may only appear dead** — recoveries after 40 min underwater. CPR during re-warming. Monitor 24 hours for secondary drowning.
- CPR: mouth-to-mouth first, then external heart massage if no pulse. Single rescuer: 30:2. Two rescuers: 30:2. Rate 100/min. Chest depression 1.5-2 inches (adults). Do not interrupt for > 5 seconds.
- AED: on each work site. Check battery daily. Use per manufacturer. Refresh skills 3-6 monthly.
- Marine animals: jellyfish, stingray, sea urchins, venomous fish, cone shells, coral, snake.
- Infectious: Weil's disease, Bilharziasis.

### Chapter 12 — Medical Emergencies Requiring Recompression
**DCI (Decompression Illness)**: Caused by inert gas bubble formation in tissues. Initial treatment: place in DDC to **60 fsw breathing 100% O2**.

**Treatment Tables**:
- **Table 6 (MANDATORY)**: Severe Type I pain, any neurological symptom, Type II symptoms, AGE at 60 fsw, blow-up with > 30 min decompression omitted. Extend at 60 f if residual symptoms after 3rd O2 period.
- **Table 6A**: Severe symptoms unchanged or worsening within first 20 min at 60 f. Compress to depth of relief (≤ 165 fsw).
- **Table 4**: Deterioration at 60 f after prompt treatment, or deemed needing deeper recompression. 120 min at 165 f. O2 at 60 f: min 4 periods (2 hrs). Both patient and tender O2 at 30 f for min 4 hrs.
- **Table 5**: Type I only, complete neuro exam normal, asymptomatic omitted decompression (< 30 min). 15-min grace period after DDC ready for medical/neuro exam. If no complete relief within 10 min at 60 f → Table 6.
- **Table 7 (HEROIC MEASURE)**: Non-responding severe AGE/life-threatening DCI. Minimum **12 hours at 60 f**. 48+ hours in chamber. Experienced medical personnel required. Temperature 85°F min. O2: 25 min + 5 min air cycles. Decompression from 60 f: 2 f per step, hold 4 hrs at 4 f, then 1 f/min to surface.
- **Table 8**: Deep blow-ups > 60 min omitted decompression. Recompression to 225 fsw max.

**Always**: Contact diving doctor, follow tables accurately, qualified tender in chamber, normal rates, examine at treatment depth, treat unconscious for AGE/Type II, observe 6 hrs post-treatment, keep patient within few minutes of DDC for 24 hrs.

**Never**: Alter tables without qualified medical direction, exceed descent rate tolerated, let patient sleep > 1 hr at any step, continue ascent if condition worsens, permit O2 > 60 feet, fail to report symptoms early, treat doubtful cases, allow cramped positions.

**DCI Terminology**: Acute, Relapsing, Neurological DCI or Acute Progressive Limb Pain and Cutaneous DCI.

**Neurological exam**: Mental status, coordination (heel-to-toe, Romberg, finger-to-nose, heel-to-shin, rapid alternating movement), extremity strength (0-5 scale), deep tendon reflexes.

**Post-treatment**: Remain near DDC 24 hours, awake every 4 hours. Approved medical practitioner examination required before return to work.

**Fitness to return to diving**:
- Type I full recovery: 24 hours
- Type I relapse: 7 days
- Type II full recovery + doctor approval: 7 days or longer
- Type II residual: **Unfit for diving**
- Pulmonary barotrauma/pneumothorax: 3 months + HRCT + diving specialist review

### Chapter 13 — Emergency & Contingency Procedures
**Emergency scenarios with step-by-step procedures:**
- Loss of breathing gas: bail-out → switch secondary → alert standby → terminate
- Loss of communication: light flash → line signals → deploy standby → terminate
- Fouled/entrapped diver: stay calm → alert standby → determine extent → free → assess
- Injured diver in water: inform supervisor → alert standby → assess nature → jump standby if needed
- Damaged umbilical: follow loss of gas and communication procedures
- Severed umbilical: bail-out → return to down-line → deploy standby → emergency decompression
- Fire/smoke on deck: inform diver → attempt to extinguish → switch secondary supply → alert standby/chamber → terminate → evacuate to off-site DDC if insufficient time
- Blow-up/emergency ascent: exhale to surface → recover → recompress to working depth → stabilise 10 min → neurological exam → Table 6 → seek medical advice from shore
- Omitted decompression: recompress immediately in DDC → Table 5 or 6
- Hot water machine failure: switch secondary → immediate decompression → prepare DDC for cold divers → terminate
- **Recovery of unconscious diver**: 17-step procedure. Key: treat for possible gas embolism on surface. DDC on-site → DDC treatment. DDC off-site → transport per ERP.
- **DCI procedures**: instigate treatment immediately. Use prefix "DIVING MEDICAL EMERGENCY" on comms. Record details on Incident Report Form.

**Chamber emergencies:**
- Fire: isolate O2 at quad, isolate electrical, transfer to adjacent lock, surface affected lock ASAP
- Loss of chamber ambient pressure: open primary/secondary supplies, transfer occupants if unresolved
- O2 leak: isolate O2 at panel, flush to ≤ 23% O2
- O2 adverse reaction: remove from O2, protect during convulsion, complete on all-air profile

**Hyperbaric abandonment:**
- Catastrophic: recover divers to surface ≤ 25 fsw/min, O2 from portable unit, surface chamber ≤ 25 fsw/min
- Major: dive abort tables, O2 from portable, controlled evacuation
- Potential major: normal decompression, essential personnel only

### Chapter 14 — Emergency Response Procedure
**Three categories**: Minor (contained onsite), Serious (requires outside support), Major (beyond onsite resources).

**Onshore ER Team**: Managing Director (Leader), Operations Manager (Deputy), Project Manager, Diving Dept Manager, HR Manager, HSE Manager.

**Offshore ER Team**: General Manager/Diving Supt (Leader), Diving Supervisor, EOC Coordinator, HSE Manager, Client Rep.

**Emergency management**: assess, inform client/management, maintain log with photos, contact emergency agencies, keep ER team informed.

**Medevac**: evacuate to nearest medical facility named in project ERP. Stabilised patient → onward to nominated hospital.

### Chapter 15 — Recruitment & Certificate Verification
**IMCA-recognised certificates**: Per IMCA D16/16. Surface-supplied and closed-bell diver certificates from ADAS, Canadian, French, Dutch, NZ, Norwegian, SA, Swedish, TSA/MSC, UK HSE, IMCA-verified. **No other certificates currently recognised.**

**Diving Supervisors**: IMCA Air or Bell Diving Supervisor Certificate only. Comparable: ADAS, DCBC, NDC (Dutch waters only).

**Verification**: Original certificates required. Check for alterations. Contact issuing body/school. Published invalidated certificates consulted.
