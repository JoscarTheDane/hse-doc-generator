# Chapter 7-9: Safety & Operational Provision, Equipment

## CHAPTER 7: SAFETY & OPERATIONAL PROVISION

### Standards
Unless specified or dispensation given by Operations Manager, all diving and deployment equipment must comply with IMCA D014, D018, DESIGN D023, D040, D064 and all associated IMCA documents, company specifications and client requirements. **Where there is conflict: more stringent requirements apply.**

### Breathing Apparatus (Diver)
- All breathing apparatus (band masks, helmets, demand valves, surface breathing equipment, BIBS masks) labelled with tag and accompanied by certificates and maintenance records
- Signed by qualified trained helmet/mask technician confirming fitness for use
- Items not so labelled when received on diving site: returned to base
- Technician must have completed suitable training course for maintenance
- User must thoroughly check all breathing equipment before each operational use
- If doubt about fitness for intended use: do not use, repair on site or return to base
- Where no suitably trained/qualified person: helmets/masks/breathing apparatus maintained by third-party company with facilities and competence
- Careful consideration to match breathing apparatus to planned work:
  - Dry helmet for most tasks (particularly HP water jetting)
  - Band mask with head protection normally used by standby diver
  - Standby diver shall wear helmet (KM37 or similar) complete with camera and light
- **All bail-out cylinders and pillar valves checked for thread compatibility using calibrated Go/No-Go gauges** — any failing removed immediately, marked "DO NOT USE"

### Maintenance
- All breathing apparatus maintained per manufacturer's technical manual
- All maintenance actions logged per PMS
- Major setting up/maintenance tasks requiring specialist equipment: conducted by competent person
- On leaving diving site: any breathing equipment returned for repair/maintenance/testing clearly marked with nature of fault or maintenance work required

### Emergency Supply (Bail-Out)
- All surface supplied divers must wear bail-out cylinder containing suitable breathing mixture for planned depth
- Duration that bail-out cylinder lasts at work depth must be calculated and included in dive plan
- **Minimum: 1 minute of breathing air for every 10 metres of umbilical from diver's nearest safe haven** (stage/basket or surface)
- Cylinder(s) must have sufficient endurance to allow diver to return to place of safety
- **Calculation uses 40 LPM as minimum consumption** — **ATLANTINE MARINE POLICY: 50 LPM** (40 + 10 LPM safety factor) — **ALL BAIL-OUT CALCULATIONS USE BREATHING RATE OF 50 LITRES PER MINUTE**

### Breathing Apparatus (Surface Control Systems)
For fire/smoke protection, breathing apparatus supplied from passive HP air source available at:
- Dive Control — panel or remote supply for diving supervisor with facility to allow communications
- Air Chamber Control — panel or remote supply for chamber supervisor
- Diving Winch Panel — remote set for winch operator with facility to allow communications
- Remote sets at suitable points to suit task (diver recovery, cross haul winch, crane, etc.)

### Chamber Operation
- Surface supplied diving range chambers operated by either qualified Diving Supervisors or trained and competent persons
- Chamber operators ensure chambers and ancillary equipment fully certified and fit for purpose
- All chambers comply with current IMCA D023 and D018 requirements and specific client requirements
- Chambers for surface decompression sited suitably close to diver's deployment device
- Access from deployment device to chamber kept clear, deck free from oil/grease/hydrocarbon
- **Chambers must have two independent means of pressurisation**
- Volumes sufficient to compress chambers at rates specified by tables in use
- Sufficient compressed air to pressurise **both chamber locks of DDC to maximum possible treatment depth** plus sufficient air to carry out **three complete surface decompression cycles** (IMCA D050)
- Sufficient oxygen on hand for:
  - Projected routine decompression
  - Two full Table 6 treatments
  - Allow one full 16 pack of oxygen as therapeutic backup
  - **Operational diving should cease when oxygen inventory falls below 90 useable cubic metres**
- **Chamber temperatures: optimum approximately 24°C (76°F)**
- **Chambers completely free of all oil, grease and other hydrocarbons** — only approved silicon-based lubricants permitted in hyperbaric environment
- No person enter chamber without first removing footwear and dirty coveralls/clothing
- Prior to chamber entry: check pockets, leave all non-essential items outside
- No matches, lighters, etc. in chambers
- Unless chamber has built-in CO2 scrubber: chambers flushed regularly to prevent CO2 build-up and increase in chamber oxygen
- **Chamber oxygen level NOT to exceed 23% at any time**

### Communications
- **Voice communications between Diver and Diving Supervisor must be recorded**
- Recording kept for **at least 48 hours**
- In event of failure: diving operation must be terminated until full communications restored
- In event of incident: recording kept unaltered until investigation satisfactorily completed
- If personnel other than Diving Supervisor need to talk directly to Diver:
  - **At no time must Diving Supervisor pass over total communications responsibility to anyone other than another properly qualified and appointed diving supervisor**
  - **At all times, Diving Supervisor must be able to hear diver's voice communications and breathing pattern**, even if another person joined into communications link
  - **Diving Supervisor must be able to disconnect all other personnel immediately** so direct link between Diver and Diving Supervisor is uninterrupted
  - Head mounted video cameras important link in communication system
  - Round-robin communication between Diving Supervisor, working Divers and Standby Diver so each can talk and hear each other when required
  - Diving Supervisor shall have **dedicated hard wire main link communications** with vessel bridge/control room, ROV Control (if SIMOPS), and crane if subsea lifting operations

### Line Signals (Royal Navy Protocol)
- All divers should employ line signals for standby/emergency communication between surface tender and diver and vice versa
- Lifeline signals: **only used when all communication systems have failed**, not as primary means
- Royal Navy protocol (not US Line-pull signals)
- Signals are combinations of pulls and bells:
  - Pull: relatively long steady tension on line
  - Bells: short tugs, given in pairs or pairs followed by remaining odd bell
  - Pair separated by short interval, longer interval to next pair or single bell
- **All signals from tender to diver preceded by one pull to attract attention**
- Signal made after diver has answered with one pull
- All signals received must be acknowledged by repeating signal until correct acknowledgement received
- Diver at work may not always acknowledge signal immediately — tender must wait before repeating
- If lifeline gets turns around down-line: impossible to get signals through, turns must be taken out from surface ASAP

### Divers' Umbilical
- Maximum diver umbilical length = length between diver's safety clip and nearest secured point of deployment (stage/basket, bell or surface platform)
- **Does not refer to secondary tending by diver or remote surface tender**
- Maximum umbilical lengths determined by projected duration of diver's bail-out and distance from tending point to nearest thruster
- **For each minute of bail-out gas available to diver, 10 metres of umbilical permitted**
- Deeper the diver descends, shorter the effective duration of bail-out, consequently shorter umbilical
- Very careful consideration ALWAYS given to proximity of thrusters, maximum umbilical calculated per IMCA D010
- Umbilicals should have 'D' ring secured at maximum permitted length (whichever is shorter as per table) by screw gate carabiner to deployment point
- **All divers' umbilicals marked at 5 metre intervals using coloured tape**
- Chart identifying markings and related position available in dive control and at tender's position
- During dive briefing: Diving Supervisor informs Diver, Standby Diver and Tender of maximum safe umbilical length
- Safe umbilical length calculated by Diving Supervisor with consideration of nearest subsea hazard, bailout duration, position of Diver Tender
- **Bail-out breathing rate: 50 LPM (40 LPM IMCA minimum + 10 LPM safety factor)**
- Umbilical length chart and marking chart in appendices

**Bail-Out Calculation Example:**
- Max depth: 38 metres
- Bail-out Volume: 10 litres
- Bail-out Charged Pressure: 170 bar (actually charged to 180 bar in cold water)
- Available Pressure = (170 - 20) bar = 150 bar
- Depth 38m = 4.8 bar + 15 bar for regulator = 19.8 bar diver cannot use
- Available for use: 150 bar × 1000 L = 15,000 litres
- At 50 LPM: 15,000 / 240 L/min = 6.25 mins
- (Note: 15 bar allowed for regulator operation, 50 litres per minute air consumption, all lengths rounded down to nearest metre)

### Diver Monitoring
- Continuous monitoring and recording of diver's condition critical to safety
- Achieved by Diving Supervisor's vigilance using:
  - Verbal communications
  - Listening to diver's breathing patterns
  - Depth monitoring using pneumofathometer
  - Visual monitoring using head-mounted cameras
  - ROV monitoring (at Supervisor's discretion)
- Standby Diver kept fully informed of diver's condition
- Best achieved by enabling standby diver to listen to Diving Supervisor and Diver's communications link
- Video monitor could be installed near Standby Diver to feed working diver's hat camera

### Diving Operations from DP DSV
**DP System Alarms Required At:** Dive control, Bridge DP desk, Main deck, Diving Superintendent's cabin, Master's cabin, Engine control room

**Dedicated primary and secondary hard wire communications** between dive control and DP desk/DP operator on bridge (open circuit comms from dive control to bridge, push to talk bridge to dive control)

**Traffic light/alarm system** in suitable position in all locations for activation and termination

**If subsea lifting operations planned:** hard wire communications link between Diving Supervisor and Crane Operator

### DP Status — Alarms and Responses

**Green — Normal Operational Status:**
- Full DP diving operations can be undertaken

**Yellow — Degraded Operational Status:**
- Flashing light and audio alarm in dive control (audio component cancelable)
- Diving Supervisor instructs divers to **suspend operations** and make safe any work/equipment that could offer further hazard before moving to agreed safe location
- After consulting with Diving Supervisor, DP Operator decides on further action (may involve divers returning to deployment device, preparing to return to surface or returning to worksite)
- If DP Operator unable to provide clear advice: Diving Supervisor instructs divers to return to deployment device and/or prepare to return to surface
- Diver safety paramount — if any doubt: DP Operator and Diving Supervisor both act for greatest protection for divers
- **During ALL air diving operations from DP DSV: deck decompression chamber must be ready for immediate use**
- **Safe location** = place that affords diver measure of protection as agreed by Diving Supervisor, Vessel Master and Diver before dive begins (typically stage/clump weight or "golden gate") — interim measure to assess nature of alert before recalling divers to surface

**Red — Emergency Status:**
- Diving Supervisor **must instruct divers to return immediately** to deployment device and/or prepare to return to surface
- After considering potential hazards (fouling of adjacent anchor wires, jacket members, etc.), deployment device and divers should be recovered as soon as possible
- DP Operator must use all available means to limit vessel position loss during recovery

### Umbilical Lengths for DP Operations
- Working diver's umbilicals must be securely tended at all times during routine operations and during any foreseeable emergency intervention
- Where excursion planned such that diver could be brought within range of physical hazard (thrusters, propellers, water intakes): umbilical must be physically restrained to prevent it coming within **5 metres** of such hazards
- Standby diver's umbilical length should be **2 metres greater** than working diver's umbilical (consider additional distance: Cstby = Cm + Extra Umbilical + LARS C/C)
- Standby diver's umbilical restrained to prevent coming within **3 metres** of any identified hazard
- Rule applies whether standby diver located on surface, mid-water or seabed
- Where deployment of standby diver and working diver from different locations: consider proximity of hazards to these locations when calculating safe umbilical lengths

### Environmental and Weather Constraints
- **No substitute for common sense** when evaluating environmental conditions
- Vessel Master, OIM or Client may require termination of diving — **only qualified diving supervisor may initiate that dive**
- Ultimate responsibility for starting or continuing diving operation rests with Diving Supervisor
- Evaluate possibility of situation deteriorating and rate of deterioration
- Commercial/contractual implications of "going down on weather" **not to play part** in decision to dive
- Dangerous environmental conditions do not lessen as commercial necessity to proceed increases

**Wind:**
- Personnel adequately protected from high temperature winds
- Standby divers and dive tenders protected from chilling effects of wind where practicable
- Effects of wind on crane jibs — local environmental guidelines for crane deployment to be adhered to
- Deployment of personnel in potentially hazardous locations to be avoided in high winds
- Deployment of baskets from rigs/platforms — care to avoid wind deflection compromising recovery
- Effects of wind on moored and DP vessels — local limits identified and logged in dive plan

**Wave:**
- Wave height (peak to trough) generally given as "maximum" and "average"
- Other factors: wave direction and period
- Standby diver deployed via different means than working diver: diving should not take place if either becomes non-viable
- Or: if diving constrained by standby mode, if two divers deployed, diving may continue if one diver returns to basket as dedicated standby
- **1.5 metre swell** maximum wave height for in-water standard air decompression
- **2 metre swell** maximum wave height for any form of in-water decompression
- Shallow water: even relatively calm sea conditions can prove exhausting — when working in shallow choppy conditions with long bottom times (180 min), divers should not become physically exhausted. Plan dive durations and manning accordingly. Head protection or dive helmet should be used in shallow water.

**Current/Tidal Limits (IMCA D067):**
- Diving in currents between **1-1.2 knots** is NOT normal operation
- Before conducting dive in 1-1.2 knot range, Diving Supervisor must:
  - Conduct thorough risk assessment to determine if safe to proceed
  - Implement appropriate risk control measures
  - Have method to constantly monitor current speed
  - If current exceeds safe limits: abort dive
- Diving in this range should not be considered unless operation has been pre-planned taking account of high current from early project stages
- Special solutions involving equipment, techniques and procedures evolved to overcome/protect diver from effects of currents and provide contingencies for foreseeable emergencies
- **Consequences of misjudging building tide can be catastrophic** — fatalities have occurred in UK North Sea through divers being caught in tidal flows preventing recovery
- **Rule: err on the safe side**
- Any consultation with diver could provide false information (location/position, peer pressure, desire to get job done)
- Diving Supervisor who has overall picture must monitor tides/currents closely

### Fog (Reduced Visibility)
- If surface visibility falls below Client prescribed distances: **over side working within 500-metre zone during diving operations must be stopped**
- Underwater activities may continue with concurrence of all parties if restrictions observed:
  - **No over side activities** from vessel: vessel maintenance by marine crew, deployment/recovery of equipment requiring removal/overreaching of vessel handrails, crane operations involving above
  - As soon as deployment/recovery complete: deck security chains should be in situ

### Ambient Light
- All worksites to be adequately lit
- Worksites planning during mobilisation phase: due provision for operating during hours of darkness
- When working over water: pool of light should encompass water surface directly below worksite
- Support vessels and pick-up boats to be equipped with search lights where reasonably practicable
- Shift supervisor responsible for maintaining safe place of work including sufficient ambient light
- Must be satisfied that realistic conditions prevail for recovering persons falling into water

### Air and Gas Requirements — Minimum Gas Quantities (IMCA D050)
- Sufficient compressed air **always available for two emergency dives** to full intended diving depth as reserve (stored in containers or from two independent and dedicated sources)
- Sufficient compressed air to pressurise **both chamber locks of DDC to maximum possible treatment depth** plus sufficient air to carry out **three complete surface decompression cycles** (stored in containers or from two independent and dedicated sources)
- **90 m³ of breathing oxygen** must be available for emergency treatment procedures

### Use of Inert Gases
- **Nitrogen** will be the only inert component of breathing mixtures (discounting trace elements in air)
- Heliox surface mixed gas diving NOT covered in this manual
- All gas cylinders identified correctly by colour code and symbol for relevant gas
- **Minimum content of oxygen in NITROX mixtures: 20%**
- Where pure inert gas utilised on worksite: procedures and permits per IMCA D070

### Diver Supply
- Gas supply at pressure commensurate with manufacturer's recommendations
- Contain oxygen percentages commensurate with tables in use
- Independent secondary backup supply immediately available on primary failure — automatically or on turn of one valve readily accessed by Diving Supervisor
- This supply introduced at umbilical termination panel (surface control panel)
- Backed up when diving from basket/stage by access to breathable wet bell atmosphere or basket bottle per IMCA D023
- Backed up with bail-out bottle charged with sufficient air/gas to permit return to wet bell, stage/basket or surface
- **Maximum PPO2 from bail-out bottle: 1.4 bar**
- **Minimum PPO2 from bail-out bottle: same as PPO2 in diver's primary breathing gas mixture, with absolute minimum of 0.4 bar**
- For surface air diving: bail-out charged with air only, volumes sufficient to allow diver to reach safety
- Check: if diver's umbilical supply fails, gas from reserve/bail-out cylinder does not exhaust into sea
- Interruption of supply to one diver does not affect other diver's supply

### Breathing Gas Quality (BS EN 12021)
- Carbon Monoxide: **5 ppm maximum**
- Carbon Dioxide: **500 ppm maximum**
- Oil: **0.5 mg/m³ maximum**
- Water: **25 mg/m³ at compressor outlet**
- Water: **50 mg/m³ at cylinder outlet (40-200 bar)**
- Water: **35 mg/m³ at cylinder outlet (over 200 bar)**
- Air shall be free from unsatisfactory odour or taste

### Purity Checks
- Air purity checks on compressors made:
  - On acceptance of equipment
  - After any significant repair or maintenance
  - At regular **6 monthly intervals**
- All premixed gas or pure oxygen supplied to worksite analysed to confirm oxygen percentage on acceptance and prior to being put on-line
- Pre-mixed gases left on static worksite for over 6 months: subjected to agitation for homogenisation
- **Sampling of all gases carried out prior to commencement of and during diving operations**

### On-Line Analysis
- Gas downstream of surface supply panel constantly analysed for oxygen content
- All warning analysers fitted with audio-visual hi-lo alarms and calibrated accordingly

### Gas Acceptance
- Gas cylinders/quads from supplier: label and purity certificate stating composition
- **Analysis of gas as received from supplier to diving installation or site is responsibility of nominated competent individual**
- **Ultimate authority for establishing exact contents of any gas cylinder, bank or unit before use rests with relevant supervisor**
- All oxygen/gas mixtures analysed after acceptance
- Deviation from specified mix/purity: check with different analyser
- Oxygen not conforming: should not be used, removed from worksite ASAP
- Gas mixtures not conforming: actual contents clearly marked on container
- Alert shift supervisor — decision on usability
- If actual contents fall outside useable parameters: gas removed from worksite ASAP
- Gas supplied via panel to diver: continuously analysed for oxygen content
- Sufficient analysers available: one analysing each diver's supply continuously, spare for regular crosschecking
- All analysers included in PMS
- All on-line analysers calibrated at **beginning of each shift** and again if unexpected readings
- Gas purity tests on routine basis per DIS No.3 and IMCA D04/11

### Gas Management — High Pressure / Low Pressure
- HP and LP gas dangerous, treat with respect
- All HP gas reduced at source unless suitable hard piping of required standard installed
- All components of HP system suitable quality and rating for job
- LP fittings must not be put on HP hoses and vice versa — if in doubt, do not fit
- No alterations/substitutions to gas circuit offshore without first consulting Diving Technician/Equipment Manager (emergency exception: as soon as practicable inform Equipment Manager and restore system)
- Only vent pressure by device specifically designed for this purpose (valve) — **never release pressure by disconnecting pipe fitting** — explosive decompression may cause serious injuries
- Pipe joints designed for internal pressures when in fixed position — failures occur when turning moment placed across them
- Pipework never used for hand/fothold
- Air/gas never used to blow areas clean (accidental introduction of high-pressure jet through human tissue may cause embolism)
- When flexible hoses used: **whip check device installed** (IMCA D065)
- No work on any pressurised pipework
- Cylinders and gas quads protected from corrosion and environmental conditions
- Prevent dirt, grit, oil or grease contaminating cylinder valves/fittings, **particularly oxygen cylinders**
- Full and empty cylinders/quads kept apart in appropriately marked areas
- All hoses correctly and safely routed
- Single cylinders secured in vertical position
- Valves neither opened nor closed without first checking with Diving Supervisor
- **All valves opened slowly and at arm's length**
- When venting gas: ear defenders/plugs and eye protection worn
- Before connecting gas supply other than air: it has been analysed
- All gas cylinders/storage containers properly colour coded and clearly labelled with gas content analysis

### Oxygen Safety
- Increasing oxygen content by only a few percent can cause rapid increase in normal combustion processes
- Material normally slightly combustible (e.g. clothing) may burn with great speed
- **All oxygen reduced to maximum 40 bar at source** (exception: on-board oxygen charging when gas must be decanted at rack pressure — circuit must be constructed/maintained to HP oxygen standards)
- Oxygen only routed through **Tungum hard piping**, flexible piping as short as practicable
- Only oxygen compatible materials used
- All piping and fittings "oxygen clean"
- Only oxygen compatible stem needle valves used on oxygen systems
- All systems and hoses for oxygen use "oxygen clean"
- Ball valves not used except as isolation valve on low-pressure side
- Sharp bends avoided
- All oxygen piping connections and oxygen supply connections on storage cylinders blanked when not in use
- **Transfer of oxygen by compressor NOT undertaken during diving operations**
- Oxygen storage cylinders stored on open deck area unless storage area approved for purpose
- Oxygen storage areas clearly marked by suitable signs
- Effective firefighting system present
- All personnel familiar with hazards of oxygen handling
- Compressed oxygen NOT used to start internal combustion engines (violent explosions may occur due to hydrocarbon/oxygen reaction)
- Smoking or hot work NOT permitted in vicinity of oxygen quads or pipe outlets
- All combustible materials (especially oil/greases) not in contact with high oxygen concentrations
- All sources of ignition safely enclosed or kept away from oxygen

### Cylinder and Fittings
**Valves:**
- Compatible with threads of equipment to be connected
- Never used as substitute for regulators (even needle valves)
- Closed sufficiently to shut off gas pressure, without excessive force

**Regulators:**
- Inlet filters to protect internal mechanism from dirt/grit (particularly oxygen regulators)
- Suitable for gas type, flow and pressure, not interchanged
- Not modified to allow bypass and increased output pressure
- Hoses not hung on them during use

**Gauges:**
- Correct gauges per manufacturer recommended
- Compatible for use with oxygen
- Appropriate for intended use
- Functionally checked within previous **six months**

**Hoses:**
- Only used for purpose designed
- Fittings checked for compatibility with hose
- LP fittings must not be put on HP hoses and vice versa
- Reusable hose end fittings marked with one, two or three rings around barrel/shoulder
- Properly attached to equipment with proprietary hose clamps (**NOT jubilee clips or tape**)
- If hose extensions essential: properly jointed using connectors
- Incorporate whip check device
- Protected from damage
- Not of excessive length
- Not coiled around cylinders and regulators when in use

### Gas Quad / Cylinder Marking
- After analysis and pressure testing: status board marked with date, contents and pressure
- All portable quads and cylinders marked **"full"** or **"empty"** — **DO NOT USE ABBREVIATION "MT"**
- All cylinders correctly colour coded per IMCA D043 and BS EN 1089-3:1997

### Compressors
**Compressor Log (mandatory for all compressors):**
- Date
- Time started pumping
- Time finished pumping
- Accumulate total number of hours run
- Date filters changed
- Name and signature of operator(s)
- Appropriate entry to planned maintenance document

**Gas Log (daily status record):**
- Date
- For each quad/cylinder: quad or cylinder number, initial pressure, initial contents analysis, final pressure, final contents analysis, contents on completion of days diving
- Name and signature of operators

### Plant and Equipment Examination, Testing and Certification
- Only approved tested and certified equipment used
- IMCA D018 with regards routine maintenance and IMCA D023 DESIGN
- HSE-DIS No. 2 and IMCA SEL 022 — wire rope integrity management
- Dive system PMS rigorously enforced to maintain required frequency of maintenance and testing
- All records maintained on site and copy in Equipment Manager's office

### Marine Signals and Identification
- All marine worksites clearly display **Flag Alpha** when divers deployed
- Local marine signal for diving operations also displayed

**International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea:**
- Three all-round lights in vertical line: highest red, middle white, lowest red
- Three shapes in vertical line: highest ball, middle diamond, lowest ball (coloured black)
- If making way through water: masthead lights, sidelights and stern light in addition
- If at anchor: anchor lights/shapes in addition to diving lights/shapes
- If obstruction exists: two all-round red lights/balls vertical line (obstruction side), two all-round green lights/diamonds vertical line (passage side)
- Rigid replica of IC flag Alpha (not less than 1 metre height) if shapes impracticable
- Vessels of less than 7 metres: not required to exhibit diving lights

### Noise Assessment
- Where required/requested: noise level assessments on worksites
- Individual items of plant assessed and rated
- Areas where noise likely to exceed threshold: designated "ear protection zones" with warning signs
- Ear defenders issued to all personnel entering ear protection zones — **wearing mandatory in designated zones**
- Wearing advised when noise levels become uncomfortable in any location
- Alternative communications arranged where appropriate when wearing ear defenders (visual warning devices for alarms, visual signals for communications)

### Water Flow Intakes, Discharges and Pressure Differentials
- Divers particularly vulnerable to suction/turbulence from water flow or pressure changes
- Dive plan must identify perceived hazard
- Precautions in risk assessment must be implemented
- Dive plan and Client's permit to dive identify discharges/intakes causing increased flow and isolation procedures
- Where isolation not possible: guards restricting flow rates to acceptable levels fitted and in good repair
- **Lock out system must be implemented on any pressure differential operational devices** (sluice gates, pumps, etc.) with **Diving Supervisor having overall management of lock out system**

**Common Hazards Precautions:**
- Platform fire pump water intakes: fit guards, isolate where possible, prevent diver going near, permit to work
- Vessel/Platform cooling water intakes: as above
- Removing blanks/equipment: double block and bleed, reduce internal pressure to ambient, secure isolation of topside valves, permit to work, pre-dive procedures to establish no subsea leakages, full drawings identifying all culverts/inlets/valves available

### Drills and Exercises
**Frequency Table:**
- Deployment of standby diver: **weekly**
- Recovery of an unconscious Diver: **prior to / during first dive of project / weekly**
- Recovery of injured Diver in water: **2 weekly**
- Loss of Divers communications: **2 weekly**
- Loss/contamination of breathing gas: **2 weekly**
- Fouled or entrapped Diver: **2 weekly**
- Fire or smoke hazard on deck: **2 weekly**
- LARS main winch / winch wire failure: **2 weekly**
- Onshore Emergency Response Team Comms: **1 monthly**

**Recording:** results recorded on Emergency Rescue Drill form, daily report, discussed at next safety meeting. Post exercise discussion/feedback encouraged. Pre-notification to deployed divers not required, but Diving Supervisor will ensure all equipment/tools made safe and announce "For exercise purposes only" on commencement.

## CHAPTER 8: MOBILE PORTABLE DIVING SYSTEMS (SRP)

### Scope
- SRP commonly referred to as SCUBA replacement package
- Used in place of complete surface supplied diving system in certain circumstances
- Advantage: readily portable, greater flexibility
- Limitation: limited supply of breathing air available
- **SRP shall NOT be used as substitute for complete surface supplied diving system**
- Generally used when diving from small craft or remote/restricted location with fully equipped SS diving system available in vicinity
- **All SRP operations to be planned as no-decompression diving activities**

### Depth Limitation
- **Maximum depth 30 metres**
- Deeper diving requires authority from Operations Manager and Safety Manager approval
- Maximum extended depth: **50 metres** — only in exceptional circumstances with risk assessment indicating acceptable
- Special consideration for deeper SRP diving: quantity of air available, proximity of decompression chamber

### Minimum Gas Requirements
- All dives planned so sufficient air available to satisfactorily complete dive including sufficient reserve in case of emergency requiring standby diver deployment
- Diving supervisor calculates gas requirements prior to each dive, recorded in dive log/record

### Decompression Chamber Facilities
- Fully equipped and IMCA DESIGN D023 / D018 compliant DDC available, located maximum **15 minutes travel time** from dive site
- To support chamber: suitable quantities of oxygen and compressed air per IMCA D050
- **15 minute time = time to have diver in chamber ready to pressurize**

### Medical Equipment
- Minimum medical equipment at support vessel for first aid and medical treatment
- In addition to standard DMAC 15 equipment: **fully functional oxygen provider set available at diving location** complete with oxygen cylinder sufficient for travel time to chamber
- Standard first aid kit also available at dive location
- Ref: DMAC 015 — Medical Equipment at Offshore Diving Operation Site

### Testing and Certification
- Equipment likely used in extreme conditions, frequent salt water immersion
- Regular inspection, maintenance and testing necessary
- All equipment tested and certified per IMCA guidelines
- Minimum annually and/or prior to using equipment: in-house or third-party audit per IMCA D040
- Non-compliant items rectified before use

### Divers' Umbilicals and Harness
- SRP/mobile/portable system NOT used where any danger of diver or umbilical becoming fouled or where immediate recovery of diver cannot be achieved
- Umbilicals marked for length per company policy and marking scheme
- Secured to divers harness by screw gate karabiner
- Diver and standby diver wear full body harness per IMCA D018

### Minimum Personnel
**Minimum team size:**
- 1 IMCA Diving Supervisor
- 2 Divers
- 2 Dive Medics
- 1 Dive Technician
- If daughter/small craft used: sixth member of dive team remains on diving support vessel/DDC location for emergency requirements
- When dive site permits (suitable size and access): additional personnel to assist with tendering
- Standby diver in immediate readiness to assist diver whenever diver in water
- All personnel suitably qualified per company's minimum diving certification standards

### Diving from Small Craft
- If dive from daughter/small craft: dedicated lookout required on support vessel
- Reliable and continuous communications between supervisor in daughter/small craft and lookout
- Second recovery craft identified and available at support vessel to assist if problem with daughter/small craft
- Daughter/small craft in close vicinity and within line of sight of support vessel
- Transit time from worksite to support vessel and to get injured diver into DDC shall not exceed **15 minutes**
- Safe and adequate means of transferring injured diver from daughter/small craft to support vessel to be established and **tested** prior to commencing operations
- Daughter/small craft to provide appropriate propulsion for fast and efficient recovery
- When diving from fixed/stable position: consider return to support vessel and DDC within limitations

### Entry and Exit of Water
- Suitable and safe means for diver and standby diver to enter and exit water
- Where freeboard is **2m or less**: dive ladder may be used
- Diver ladder must extend **below water surface by 2m**, have suitable steps and hand holds
- Consideration for suitable means to recover injured/unconscious diver out of ladder

### Environmental Considerations
- **Visibility**: only in conditions of good surface visibility, daylight hours only (unless small craft has electrical power generation for lighting), normal marine emergency equipment (torches, flares, etc.)
- **Sea-state**: consideration given to location from which dive takes place, launch and recovery from support vessel, during diving operation
- **Currents**: vary with location and surface currents affected by wind, risk assessment must account for effect on type of work and depth of dive

## CHAPTER 9: SAFE USE OF EQUIPMENT

### 9.1 Underwater HP Jetting
- Effective method of removing marine growth and cleaning metal surfaces underwater
- Even minor incidents have potential to cause severe injuries
- Equipment carefully selected for suitability, inspected/checked by competent person before use
- **ALL OPERATORS TRAINED AND FULLY BRIEFED**
- IMCA D049 Code of Practice must be implemented, plus risk assessments and checklists
- Ref: DMAC 003, IMCA D049

**Equipment:**
- High Pressure Pumping Unit: continuous operation using filtered seawater, constructed for marine environment
- Dump Safety Gun: fail-safe valve diverts water to ambient water when trigger released (severe jolt experienced — choke can be fitted to reduce effect)
- Dry Shut Off Gun: pressure retained in supply line when trigger released, used with regulating/unloading valve on surface
- Trigger Mechanism: cam lever operated, returns to safe position on release (fatigue from maintaining "open" position over extended periods)
  - **SAFETY: Locking trigger in "open" position by wire, tape or artificial means is EXTREMELY DANGEROUS and must NOT be carried out under any circumstances**
  - Safety catch provided capable of immobilising gun in "off" position
- End Connections: high erosion/corrosion, check at regular intervals, whip checks fitted
- Lance: inherent potential for self-injury increases as lance length decreases
- Nozzles: combinations of forward/backward facing nozzles to balance thrust
- Retro Jets: **ALL HP water guns handled by divers underwater must be fitted with retro jet**
  - Nozzle must provide reaction equal to lance to balance gun
  - Retro jets must be guarded with venturi type diffuser tube, either positively locked onto gun or if removed, releases retro nozzle from gun to avoid injury
- High Pressure Hose: not used above SWP, clearly marked, couplings minimised, non-corroding material, pressure rating at least equal to hose, different thread to prevent connection of different pressure rated hoses

**Care & Maintenance:**
- Daily check on pump and fittings for damage/leaks by competent person
- HP hoses, guns and lances examined for damage
- If leaks/damage found: report to Diving Supervisor, withdraw equipment pending repair
- Internally check fittings and interconnecting hoses at regular intervals (disconnection, visual inspection)
- Thorough flush with fresh water on demobilisation or prior to extended storage
- Six-monthly testing of pump unit and associated equipment by competent person with certificate of fitness
- Where hired: certificate valid for no more than 6 months from date of test by hirer
- Full operating and maintenance instructions supplied with each pump, strictly adhered to

**Operational Procedures:**
- **At no time more than one diver working in area where jetting is taking place or likely to take place**
- No jetting except on instructions from Diving Supervisor
- Near surface/air-water interface: care to ensure no other personnel in area, area kept clear of workboats
- Access to working areas strictly prohibited while jetting in progress and until work completed and pump shut down
- Signal to start pump only given by diver when in working position and ready to start (warn about to receive pressure)
- **ONLY SUPPLY HIGH PRESSURE WATER ON DEMAND FROM DIVER**
- Working area kept free from tripping hazards, adequate lighting provided

**Handling the Gun:**
- Diver must **always use two hands** to control gun — majority of accidents occur because diver fails to control with both hands
- Diver must ensure stable position before starting jetting
- Method of stabilising by platform or trapeze should always be considered
- When rearranging position, trigger must be released and safety catch applied until stability re-established
- Diver always aware of umbilical location and clear to surface/stage at all times

**Pump must be shut down and safety catch applied:**
- Whenever diver has completed immediate task and relocating
- Whenever gun laid down on worksite or seabed
- At end of dive prior to deployment of second diver
- During change round of divers
- During diver change out (if on DP, equipment recovered to surface unless turnaround immediate)
- During all diver movements
- During recovery of gun to surface

- Diver must confirm with Diving Supervisor that requests to activate/shut down pump have been carried out
- Diving Supervisor must always confirm and log that diver's requests for activation/shut down have been carried out
- During use: ensure retro jet diffuser tube not blocked by debris (may cause serious injury if deflected)

**DP Acoustic Interference:** Noise from HP jetting may interfere with acoustic reference system of DP ship — DP operators informed when jetting about to start

**Communications:**
- Audio communications usually difficult during actual jetting
- Diving Supervisor must monitor diver's jetting operations and breathing rates, act quickly if rhythm or sudden change in performance observed
- Diving Supervisor **must ALWAYS be in direct and immediate contact** with technician running pump — who must be in position to rapidly shut down at all times when diver using HP equipment
- Noise interference from adjacent machinery must NOT affect communications between Diving Supervisor and pump operator
- **No underwater jetting unless these conditions can be met**

**Injuries:**
- HP water jet injection can cause extensive tissue damage and severe infection
- Danger may exist even if skin only slightly broken or not at all
- **Every injury caused by HP water jetting must be reported and treated, medical advice sought immediately**
- Record details: "This man has been involved with high pressure water jetting at pressures up to 14,500 lb/in² (100 MPa, 1000 Bar, 1019 kg/cm²) with jet velocity of 900 miles (1440 km) per hour. Please take this into account when making your diagnosis. Unusual infections with micro aerophilic organisms occurring at lower temperatures have been reported. These may be gram negative pathogens, such as are found in sewage. Bacterial swabs and blood cultures may therefore be helpful."

### 9.2 Safe Use of Lifting Equipment & Buoyancy Aids
- Covers: removal of heavy objects off seabed, mid-water, from platforms/jetties/piers, use of cranes/Winches underwater, man-riding winches
- All lifts require formal Lift Plans and Risk Assessments approved before conducting lift

**Cranes:**
- Operating limits for load, extension and environmental conditions must NEVER be exceeded
- Secure communications: Crane Driver/Operator, ROV Supervisor, Deck Foreman, Diving Supervisor, Bridge, and between Diving Supervisor and diver(s)
- **Only Diving Supervisor may issue instructions to Crane Driver**

**Co-ordinated Activities (Crane/Surface Rig):**
- Before weight placed on crane/winch: Diver double-checks lifting wire strops, shackles, hooks etc., reports to Diving Supervisor configuration of lifting gear and confirms integrity
- Diver attaches to lift points and informs Diving Supervisor to take strain
- All safety devices (shackle pins, carne hook locking devices) in place and secured
- Tag line secured to item being lifted for use by Diver and deck/surface crew
- Divers position themselves to see load, in safe position, ensure tethers/umbilicals clear
- Load lifted clear of seabed/obstructions nominally **5 metres**, then lift stopped
- Lift boomed clear in preparation for recovery
- Consideration in RA to need to recover diver during lifting (complexity, weight, material — e.g. tubulars)
- **No crane movements without approval from Diving Supervisor**

**Diver Controlled Lifts (Lift Bags):**
- Parachute type (open at bottom)
- Relief valves and/or inverter lines used to deflate
- Main problem: uncontrolled ascent of bag or bag+item — can cause injury/damage
- **Ref: IMCA D016**

**Precautions for Lift Bags:**
- Only certified rigging used as hold back lines — **holdbacks to be used at all times**, attached to item and suitable clump-weight/structure to restrain in event of uncontrolled ascent
- Consideration to additional weight caused by suction
- All associated rigging and parachutes certified or identified on letter of conformity
- Inverter lines used and attached to top of lift bag and suitable anchor point
- Deck crew visually check each item before use for leaks/damage
- In strong tidal areas: air bags deflated before diving suspended due to strong currents
- If on DP: bridge informed that air emissions may interfere with HPR reference system
- Lift of bag to correspond closely to weight of lift to reduce excessive loading on hold back lines
- **Divers must ensure at all times they are clear of load and umbilicals not fouled by load or lift bag rigging**
- Divers and supervisors aware of Boyle's Law principles when using lift bags — extreme caution in shallow water (0-30m)
- **SAFETY: Lift bags are NOT to be used as test weight for load testing on surface** — design is lifting device, if inverted possibility of failure

### 9.3 Safe Use of Cutting and Welding Equipment

**Underwater Oxy Arc Cutting:**
- **SAFETY NOTE: Numerous deaths occurred during underwater oxy-arc cutting/burning operations. Coldcut techniques should be reviewed as alternative and oxy-arc used as last resort.**
- Conductor for amperage to cutting torch (- amps): minimum **95mm²** or American 3-0
- Return side (+ amps): minimum **70mm²** or American 4-0
- No unauthorised modifications to cutting torch, only company-supplied equipment
- Amperage controlled by **double pole single throw knife switch** in negative (-amp) circuit (preferred) or electric knife switch with double pole isolation
- This switch only operated during cutting operations by Diving Supervisor or delegated person
- Switch rigged within easy reach of Diving Supervisor at dive controls or sighted from position (either open or closed)
- **Manual knife switch NOT permitted for use**
- Diver assessed as competent to carry out underwater cutting before commencing
- Diver requests current adjustment by voice communications with Diving Supervisor
- If doubt about order or communication lost: circuit broken immediately
- Circuit broken routinely: when new rod placed in torch, when stub of used one removed, whenever diver changing position, whenever cutting rig recovered/deployed, on completion of job

**Precautions:**
- Divers do not position themselves between earth clamp and cutting torch
- **At no time must diver become part of earth return circuit**
- Wear rubber gloves under normal dive gloves
- All maintenance/remedial works on surface
- Do not cut into compartment without ascertaining what is on other side
- Vent inflammable gases generated by arc which may become trapped in enclosed compartment — cut vent hole first, test with pneumofathometer gas supply to ensure adequate venting
- Ensure electrical supply is earthed
- Never hold cutting torch so it is held towards body
- Ensure pieces of cut material and slag do not fall on diver or umbilical — large pieces secured by suitable rigging
- Divers must wear **full helmet (not band mask)** with suitable lens to protect eyes
- Diving helmets checked frequently for possible electrolytic corrosion

**Preparation:**
- Entire rig removed from stowage and laid out on deck so it does not overlap (increases efficiency by avoiding dense magnetic fields from energised power cable)
- Cutting rig tested prior to deployment — check polarity by connecting earth clamp to metal plate immersed in seawater, immerse torch with rod tip a few inches from plate, when current applied, stream of bubbles from rod indicates correct polarity
- Cutting gear firmly secured to strong point on vessel/structure after ensuring diver has sufficient length
- Cutting rigs are heavy items — if run over side out of control: may fall on/divert/diver pull away
- Ensure cutting rig and current conductor not snagged, crushed, deformed or broken
- Particular care when led over sharp edge introducing acute angle in cable
- Always check electrical continuity across components — components failing continuity test not to be used
- Protect cutting cable from abrading or interfering with insulation to current conductors
- Personnel should avoid stepping on cutting cable
- Only use high flow regulator such as Tescom 1000 for oxygen supply
- Ensure regulator and umbilical hose is Oxygen cleaned
- Only use high grade purity oxygen supplies
- If cutting "load": ensure load will not fall and injure/trap diver once cut

**Post Dive Procedure:**
- On completion: cutting rig thoroughly rinsed with fresh water
- Visual inspection of whole rig for obvious breaks in outer insulation
- Oxygen turned off, cutting rig drained to atmosphere by valve on torch
- Cutting torch stripped down, rinsed in fresh water, thoroughly dried by lint free rags
- Thorough visual inspection of torch connections and internal seals
- On disconnection: oxygen hose and torch sealed in polythene bag, tagged with date and fitness for purpose
- Where possible, cutting rig stored off deck and away from contamination sources (exhaust fumes, drilling mud)
- Cutting rig length and function clearly marked
- On completion and stripping down: oxygen regulator removed from oxygen source, sealed in polythene bag, stowed in oil free place
- Oxygen gas storage outlet plugged

**Ref:** IMCA D003, OGP 471

**Underwater Welding Safety Precautions:**
- Electrical power source must be insulated (both high and low voltage DC)
- Check polarity
- Welding machine frame must be earthed before start of operations
- Use only torches specifically made for underwater use
- Torches and joints in cable properly insulated
- Electrode only changed/tightened when no current in circuit
- Never let any metallic part of diving equipment contact electrode
- Current off at all times except when actually welding — circuit not closed unless specifically directed by diver welder
- Rubber gloves always worn during wet welding
- Appropriate grade of welding filter to protect eyes
- Always inspect metallic parts of diving gear for electrolysis
- When working in enclosed area: make certain no escaped gases trapped
- Do not weld if hazardous materials in vicinity
- Be mindful of electronic systems on subsea manifolds/wellheads which may be damaged by electric welding currents

### 9.4 Electricity Underwater
- Diver in water very susceptible to electric shock
- Salt water better conductor than body — much less risk than in fresh water
- Electricity causes local tissue damage (burns, blisters at entry site) and paralysis
- If respiratory or cardiac muscles affected: results may be fatal
- Whenever practicable, all underwater equipment complies with IMCA D045
- **DC will be employed where possible** for underwater use
- AC or AC rectified generators may be used when fitted with suitable safety devices
- RCD and isolation transformers with insulation monitors should be installed to minimise risk

**Working on Electrical Conductors/Appliances:**
- Before any work planned: detailed work procedures produced with Client/owners to identify requirements and availability of active protective devices in circuit
- All electrical appliances/conductors must be isolated by competent electrician per prescribed procedures
- Where reasonably practicable, circuit tested after isolation with suitable instrument at site of work
- Lock out/tag out systems to be used

### 9.5 Safe Use of Inflatable Craft Operations
**Weather Limits:** common sense principal guideline — deployment not to take place when weather/visibility not conducive to safe deployment

**Minimum Equipment:** waterproofed radio, first aid pack, pyrotechnic pack, spare tank and lead, torch, pack of light sticks, sea anchor and suitable line, two paddles, emergency tool kit, inflation device, gas horn, radar reflector

**Safety Procedures:**
- Personnel to board inflatable once in water and secured to vessel — not to "ride" boat down (unless specific arrangements/procedures made)
- When landing on piers/jetties/platforms/others: only designated landing points used
- Permission to launch/recover from bridge duty officer, diving supervisor, or platform control officer/OIM
- List of inflatable crew/personnel kept on bridge/control room
- Radio watch on relevant channel maintained whilst personnel deployed
- **Minimum crew of two** to remain in inflatable when deployed

**Small Craft Operator — Coxswain:**
- Appointed by Senior Diving Supervisor/Diving Superintendent
- Competence with small boat operation and familiarity with deployment procedures confirmed
- Issued with company Certificate of Competence, signed by Senior Diving Supervisor/Diving Superintendent
- Copy sent to HR Officer and placed in individual's personnel file
- Where national requirements exist for formal training/certification: must be in place prior to operating craft

### 9.6 Use of Epoxy by Divers
- Uncured epoxy resins produce several toxic vapours and present significant hazard in surface and hyperbaric saturation conditions
- **As little as 50 grams will present significant inhalation hazard**
- Before any worksite uses epoxy resin: detailed working procedure produced and submitted to safety department for approval
- Specification and data sheets (COSHH Manual) of active components and solvent obtained before procedures produced

**Working Procedures:**
- Diver and standby apply silicone-based barrier cream to all skin areas likely to contact resin
- Diver wears disposable coveralls and industrial rubber gloves when working with resin or equipment associated with resin
- In event of contamination: diver jettisons coveralls into suitable container for disposal on surface
- Separate downline dedicated for all equipment associated with resin — not to be used for deployment of other material/equipment
- All topside personnel take similar measures to avoid contamination to themselves and diver's equipment

**Decontamination (Interior of DDC):**
- All contaminated clothing removed before entering chamber
- If contamination took place: diver breathes from BIBS in chamber and notifies Diving Supervisor
- Chamber operator informs Diving Supervisor who takes appropriate action to safeguard diver and remove contaminated items
- **Under no circumstances must cleaning solvent be admitted to chamber whilst under pressure**

**Symptoms of Resin Intoxication:**
- Narcosis, irritation of eye, throat, nose, asthma, dermatitis
- **Treatment:** obtain medical advice giving full details of resin components (as on material data sheets), remove diver from further risk of contamination

### 9.7 Simultaneous ROV and Air Diving Operations
- ROV and diving operations deemed "combined" when:
  - ROV and divers operating from same vessel
  - ROV and divers working on same structure (pier, cofferdam, platform, drilling rig, vessel, etc.) — irrespective of parent vessel
  - ROV and diver may intercept each other due to operating within separation distance less than combined lengths of both ROV and diver umbilicals

**Responsibilities:**
- ROV Supervisor: ensures ROV configured in state safe for divers as reasonably practicable, completes ROV element of project RA, ensures known defect/weakness in ROV's operating capacity brought to attention of Diving Supervisor before operations commence, ensures pilots competent and suitably qualified to safely control ROV in combination with diving operations
- ROV Pilot: immediately inform ROV Supervisor of doubt/concern with ability to conduct task, immediately inform diving supervisor/control of any fault/alarm/ROV system defect during combined operations
- Diving Supervisor: ensures all divers aware of potential hazards of ROV(s), ensures emergency procedures for recovery of ROV agreed with ROV Supervisor/Pilot and understood by diving personnel, **authorises ROV to be dived, recovered and leave tethered management system** (only with authority of Diving Supervisor and vessel master/OIM while diving in progress, precautions to avoid umbilical fouling), coordinates all diver and ROV movement

**Ultimate responsibility:** Combined ROV and diving operations — ultimate responsibility of Diving Superintendent, delegated to each shift Diving Supervisor
- ROV Supervisor responsible for operation and welfare of ROV and operating crew
- **Dual responsibility** of Diving Superintendent/Supervisor and ROV Supervisor: ensure respective operating crews fully briefed on diving plant, ROV systems, dangers, limitations, operating parameters
- **Dual responsibility:** ensure respective crews fully briefed and understand tasks, operating areas, intended movements prior to deployment of both ROV and divers
- **Dual responsibility:** ensure good lines of communications between each other and other operations personnel, each party receives immediate communiqué regarding unusual/non-procedural situations or emergencies
- Risk management process includes positioning of ROV while in water (above or below diver working area), consideration in event of ROV power failure — ROV will not affect diver's umbilical

**Communications:**
- Effective communications essential
- Communication must exist between Shift Diving Supervisor and ROV Supervisor
- **Shift Diving Supervisor has ultimate responsibility for combined operation**
- Without effective communications: supply of vital resources may be interrupted, unsafe practices adopted
- ROV Supervisor must be given reasonable access to vessel/installation communications service
- ROV Company to provide **dual-channels/two-way hard wired communications** between ROV control room, ROV launch site and dive control room
- Vessel/Diving Contractors to provide adequate communications between ROV control room and bridge
- Communication checks pre-dive and at regular intervals during working dive
- Lost/failed communications: **STOP call** made and rectification before recommencement
- Emergency backup communication system (UHF/VHF/General telephone system) provision

**ROV Deployment/Recovery:**
- May comprise: hydrostatic ROV release unit, single boom swivel davit, power winch (air or electric), winch wire, umbilical sheave
- Sighting criteria: shall NOT be sited where deployment/recovery will impede safe passage of divers from diving ladder, stage/basket or wet bell to DDC
- Shall NOT be sited where deployment/recovery will hamper visual monitoring of diving operations by dive control station
- Shall NOT be sited where ROV operations may cause damage/hindrance to diving equipment (airlines, compressors, hot water machines, decompression chambers, etc.)
- Power cables/air hoses preferably run such that they do not cross or interfere with those used for diving operations
- Deployment/recovery only after:
  - Permission granted by Diving Supervisor/Diving Superintendent via operating ROV Pilot
  - Deployment/recovery operator visually checked over side to ensure winch wire clear of all diving umbilicals, down lines, rigging, etc.
- **Shift diving supervisor has ultimate control and responsibility** for combined ROV and diving operation

**Deployed ROV:**
- ROV Pilot should ensure fully briefed and understands location of divers and intended movements
- Divers should ensure fully briefed and understand location of ROV and intended movements
- Both periodically check around to ensure not in close proximity, nor danger of crossing each other's umbilicals leading to entanglement
- Increased vigilance in conditions of diminished visibility or increasing currents
- When ROV and divers working in very close proximity (e.g., ROV monitoring diving), ROV pilot should maintain visual contact with diver at all times
- If visual sight of diver lost: ROV and shift diving supervisors informed, best method of regaining visual contact instigated
- ROV thrusters may pose no real threat to diver, but diver briefed on location and remain clear of thrusters
- **Thruster guards to be fitted when deployed with divers in water**
- Divers briefed on damage that hand-tools, knives, etc. can exact on ROV thrusters, and sharp objects cutting into vehicle's umbilical

**Umbilical Entanglement:**
- If ROV pilot suspects diver's umbilical entangled with ROV:
  - **Make NO ATTEMPT to recover entangled vehicle to launch unit or surface**
  - **Immediately inform Shift Diving Supervisor at dive control** via comms system or messenger in event of comms failure — **on no account relinquish vehicle control**
  - Attempt to park or stabilise vehicle on low thruster power
  - If vehicle safely parked, thrusters switched off, maintaining visual via video camera only
  - Inform Shift Diving Supervisor that vehicle is stable
  - Then it is Diving Supervisor's responsibility to instruct diver to free entangled umbilicals, maintaining continuous communications with ROV Pilot
  - When "all clear" given by shift Diving Supervisor/Diving Superintendent, ROV may resume operations or be recovered

### 9.8 Safe Use of Dredging and Seabed Stabilisation Equipment
**Excavation of Seabed & Spoil Heaps:**
- Two main diver operated methods: Air lifting and Subsea dredging
- Air lifting: pumping low pressure air down to lift tube, expands within tube causing suction (by differential pressure) at bottom, discharging spoil at top. Air flow controlled at air lift by diver operating quarter turn valve.
- Subsea dredging: water venturi system or surface supplied hydraulic system driving subsea pump. Both have handheld suction nozzle with diver controlled quarter turn valve.
- **Precautions:**
  - Planning and RA: consider extent of excavation and soil types, requirements for shuttering
  - Good communication between diver and Diving Supervisor at all times
  - Diver to ensure hands, feet, equipment (such as umbilicals) not pulled into suction heads
  - Compressors and pumps should be made cold prior to divers attempting to clear blockage at suction head
  - Discharged spoil directed away from working area in manner deposited in area clear of diver's equipment and subsea installations
  - Monitor spoil fall in case of airlift
  - When using large crane supported type of airlift: consider roll of vessel which may induce unacceptable rise and fall of airlift — critical when excavating pipelines as airlift may make uncontrolled contact with line and cause damage
  - DSV Bridge informed before start of air lifting
  - Expanding air bubbles can and frequently do interfere with subsea positioning reference systems

**Installation of Protection Mattresses:**
- Mattresses used to protect pipelines/spools or stabilise in position
- Various types: concrete segments/bars held together by ropes or bitumen encased in canvas
- Made to conform to specific design criteria, varying in size and weight

**Mattress Deployment:**
- Installed either with purpose-built frame (generally provided by mattress supplier) or lifting beam
- Frame has lifting strops along each side, weight distributed evenly along it when hoisted off deck
- Once in position over pipeline: strops released by diver and frame recovered

**Preparation:**
- All lifting aids fully certified and documentation available on vessel/site
- White centre line painted on mattress (if not already by supplier) — aids positioning by diver, results in fewer crane movements, safer operation
- All frame-lifting strops attached to mattress providing even lift
- Darkness or bad visibility: light sticks or strobes attached to strategic points of load as diver visual aids
- Weighted tag lines attached from either end of mattress to aid diver movement/positioning

**Precautions:**
- Due regard to sea state and splash zone through which mattresses deployed
- Excessive wave loading can cause rigging to fail with subsequent danger to personnel and equipment
- Deck crew carry out checks as for "Deployment of Loads Subsea"
- Divers clear, ensure umbilicals have slack taken up by tender
- Load stopped with bottom of mattress **3 metres above seabed**
- When divers position mattress: ensure umbilicals do not become trapped, second diver can tend if necessary
- After positioning mattress, frame lowered onto it so it does not impact on diver when releasing lifting strops
- Each lifting strop hooked back onto itself to avoid snagging when recovering frame
- **Mattress installation is frequent cause of diver hand injuries** — divers well briefed on techniques and hazards before commencing

**Sand/Gravel/Grout Bag Emplacement:**
- Standard bag size: 25 kg, made from polypropylene or hessian
- **No bag heavier than this should be handled by any single individual**
- Deployed individually or in 1 tonne bulk bags containing 40 sandbags (normal "Deployment of Loads Subsea" precautions apply)
- **Precautions:**
  - In poor visibility: danger of bags being placed over diver's umbilical
  - Both divers and deck crew reminded to use correct posture when lifting by hand to avoid personal injury and back strains

### 9.9 Safe Use of Personnel Transfer Systems

**Personnel Basket:**
- Standard basket constructed for maximum capacity of **4 persons** (larger units available)
- Equipped with bumpers to protect persons from injury
- Stabilisers and shock chords provided for stability
- **Tested every 6 months with test load of twice maximum working load** (may be performed on platform under supervision of OIM/Master or designee)
- Use approved by Master/OIM/Project Manager and Client
- Transfer voluntary on part of each individual
- Used **only in cases where no other means of access is available**
- Transport of personnel to/from vessel **only when absolutely necessary**
- **Daylight hours only**
- Crane operated by qualified person
- No one transferred unless wearing lifejacket secured to body in approved manner
- Personnel only allowed to ride on **outside of basket and must be facing inwards**
- Inside of basket: minor baggage and gear only
- Proper lift/landing area kept clear at all times
- During whole transportation: Vessel's Master, crane operator and responsible person onboard platform in direct radio contact
- **Rescue boat ready for immediate use**
- Raising/lowering preferably above sea and not above vessel or platform
- Officer in charge responsible for visual inspection of all personnel prior to transfer

### 9.10 Safe Use of Hydraulic Tools
- Danger from hydraulic pressures at tool minimal
- Only small amount of fluid loss required to remove all power if pressure accidentally released
- Hydraulic tools provide very strong torque outputs — diver to ensure equipment does not become entangled
- **All hydraulic tools must be fitted with "dead man" switches** — carefully checked for function before each dive
- Any change of disk, tool bit or tool: carried out on surface
- **Under no circumstances are such switches to be tied back or modified**

### 9.11 Explosives
- Explosives use during diving operations is specialist task requiring correct training and procedures
- Not in scope of this manual
- If explosives required: complete procedure, risk assessment and training provided as part of project planning
