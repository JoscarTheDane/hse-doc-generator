# Chapters 13-15: Emergency & Contingency Procedures, Response, Recruitment

## CHAPTER 13: SURFACE SUPPLIED DIVING EMERGENCY & CONTINGENCY PROCEDURES

### Introduction
- Cardinal rule: **"DO NOT PANIC"** — actions must be quick AND correct, correct decisions not made without complete grasp of situation
- If diver in emergency: take time to assess situation, determine correct action and what to do to help self, whilst still giving Diving Supervisor clear concise report of status as conditions allow
- Diving Supervisor needs all facts available to handle situation with full support of topside crew — automatically gives confidence to diver in compromised situation
- All members of diving team and anyone with direct/indirect working relationship with diving team must read and understand this chapter — knowledge could play vital part in saving a life
- Emergency training matrix established for each project

### 13.1.2 Loss of Breathing Gas (or Contamination of Breathing Gas)
1. Diver to go on bailout supply and inform Supervisor
2. Supervisor to switch to secondary supply and inform diver
3. Alert standby diver
4. Air to working diver's pneumo and insert pneumo to working diver's mask (only if secondary supply fails)
5. Diver to return to down-line/stage
6. Deploy standby diver to assist (as required)
7. Terminate dive (following normal decompression procedures)

### 13.1.3 Loss of Communication
1. Repeated flashing of diver's hat mounted light (if no hat light, revert to step 2)
2. Supervisor notifies tender to signal diver with umbilical pulls to abort dive
3. If positive response, continue with umbilical signals until diver recovered to surface (decompression to continue as per normal)
4. Alert standby diver
5. Diver to return to down-line or stage/basket (if deployed to working depth) and await standby
6. Deploy standby diver/advise him to establish visual contact with working diver
7. Standby diver: situation assessment
8. Diver to surface (ascent and stops controlled through standby diver communication) following normal decompression procedures
9. Terminate operations until communications fault located and rectified

### 13.1.4 Fouled or Entrapped Diver
1. Stay calm and inform Supervisor of problem
2. Alert standby diver
3. Diver to determine extent of entrapment and attempt to free himself
4. Deploy standby diver as required
5. When diver free: assess situation and take appropriate action (terminate dive)

### 13.1.5 Injured Diver in Water
1. Inform Diving Supervisor of injury
2. Alert Standby Diver and Tenders
3. Determine nature and extent of injury
4. If required: Jump the Standby Diver
5. Standby Diver to follow unconscious Diver's umbilical, giving running commentary of actions as proceeds to unconscious Diver
6. Standby Diver on arrival at stricken Diver: accurately assess situation, report to Diving Supervisor
7. Administer first aid (where possible), assess situation, evaluate injury, Standby to remain with diver
8. Take up slack in both divers' umbilicals, made ready to assist in bringing both divers to surface

### 13.1.6 Damaged Umbilical
- Follow loss of air supply and loss of communications procedures (13.1.2 and 13.1.3)

### 13.1.7 Severed Umbilical
1. Diver onto bail-out
2. Air to standby diver's pneumo (as requested)
3. Return to down-line/stage/basket and activate onboard gas cylinder supply
4. Deploy standby diver
5. Commence emergency decompression
- If second standby diver's rig available and "on-line": consideration should be given to deploying it with standby diver

### 13.1.8 Fire or Smoke Hazard on Deck/In Control Van
1. Diving Supervisor immediately informs diver of situation to prepare to leave bottom
2. One of dive team/deck crew attempt to extinguish fire
3. Switch divers supply to secondary HP supply — **only if diver's main supply from LP compressor**
4. Alert standby diver (ensure on HP supply) and deck crew to be ready DDC to recover diver. If DDC onsite: inform chamber operator to prepare chamber
5. Diver to return to downline or stage
6. BA masks made ready to use (mask does not need to be worn until need arises)
7. Determine what decompression diver required to serve, if any
8. Terminate dive and recover diver
9. **If decompression required and insufficient time to do in-water decompression**: assess situation and **evacuate to nearest off-site DDC**. Place diver on oxygen and activate ERP.
10. Isolate all HP supplies at source
- **Note: Step 5 decision point** — if decompression required, Diving Supervisor to decide based on location of fire whether to commit to onsite decompression or to evacuate diver per ERP. If evacuating: diver must be accompanied by Diver Medic along with oxygen administration kit.

### 13.1.9 Blow Up or Emergency Ascent
1. Exhale all the way to surface
2. Recover umbilical as quickly as possible
3. Remove diver from water
4. Recompress diver to equivalent working depth in chamber and allow to stabilise for 10 minutes (if AGE suspected, recompress to 18m with tender present, carry out neurological exam and decompress on USN Table 6)
5. **SEEK MEDICAL ADVICE FROM SHORE**

### 13.1.10 Omitted Decompression: Surface Supplied Diving
- If divers decompression compromised: recompress immediately in DDC
- Compress to appropriate depth for treatment (USN Table 5 or Table 6)
- If no ill effects: decompress in accordance with treatment table
- Any ill effect or recurrence: shift to appropriate table and seek medical advice

### 13.1.11 Hot Water Machine Failure
1. Inform diver
2. Diver to return to down-line/stage
3. Switch to secondary water supply (if applicable)
4. Commence immediate decompression (**omitted decompression must be considered if no hot water backup available**)
5. Prepare DDC for cold divers: raise temperature, provide woolen underclothes, may require a tender
6. Terminate operations until fault resolved (or change mode of operation to dry suit/wet suit if suitable)

### 13.1.12 Recovery of Unconscious Diver
1. No contact with Diver despite attempting to attract attention with comms, light and line pull signals. Diver assumed unconscious.
2. Switch to secondary supply, monitoring line pressure for obvious signs of pressure loss
3. Make any tools being used "COLD"
4. Jump the Standby Diver and alert Chamber Operator to ready chamber (DDC)
5. Standby to follow unconscious Diver's umbilical, releasing any umbilical restraints, giving running commentary of actions as proceeds to unconscious Diver
6. Standby Diver on arrival at stricken Diver: accurately assess situation, report to Diving Supervisor
7. Standby diver opens free flow, checks and reports pressure of bail-out of diver. Reports status of consciousness/breathing, injuries.
8. If no surface supply air is flowing: turn off free flow and open bailout side block valve, check flow to regulator
9. Take up slack in both divers' umbilicals, made ready to assist in bringing both divers to surface
10. LARS winch/davit operator to make ready diving stage/recovery device at suitable depth to enable safe recovery to deck
11. Standby Diver recovers unconscious diver to stage/recovery device while monitoring and reporting state of diver, on ascent keep unconscious diver's airway open to ensure exhale
12. On arrival at stage/recovery device, Standby Diver:
    - Place unconscious diver in stage/davit sling
    - Attach security lanyard to unconscious diver's harness and recovery will be made
    - Standby diver to support unconscious diver's head to retain open airway and protect spine from damage during ascent
13. Standby Diver to communicate to dive control, clear to surface and recover
14. If using a stage: Standby diver will accompany diver to surface at required ascent rate ignoring any in-water stops and monitoring condition throughout
15. **DDC ONSITE**: upon arrival at surface DMT to take charge and excuse people not needed. DMT & Tenders to unhook unconscious Diver from stage/recovery device and assist with stretcher to putting into DDC.
16. **DDC OFFSITE**: upon arrival at surface DMT to take charge and excuse people not needed. DMT & Tenders to unhook unconscious Diver from stage/recovery device and place diver in "lay down" position with legs extended. Maintaining clear airway place on oxygen supply and ensure breathing. **Transport to nearest DDC per ERP, action the ERP.**
17. Unconscious diver, DMT & standby diver (if required), will be recompressed to maximum working depth of dive where standard recovery procedures followed to establish diver's vital signs (assuming worst case of non-breathing unconscious patient). After patient's condition stabilised: decision made whether to proceed with therapeutic table (USN 6) or further medical investigations as dictated by medical advice.

**Note: Whenever an unconscious diver is recovered, upon reaching surface he must be treated for possible gas embolism. Diver being treated for possible gas embolism shall be accompanied by DMT tender during all treatment inside chamber.**

### 13.2.0 Decompression Illness Procedures
- Medical emergencies fall into two categories: Diving (pressure related) and Post-diving
- Atlantic Marine policy: each diving team to include sufficient number of Diver Medic Technicians to cover worksite for duration of project
- All divers shall have first aid training

**Procedure:**
- If diver shows signs/symptoms of DCI: instigate treatment immediately, irrespective of all other factors
- Professional medical assistance and advice readily available (contact info in ERP)
- In radio/telephone communication regarding diving incident: use prefix **"DIVING MEDICAL EMERGENCY"**
- Relevant facts and ensuing actions recorded while details fresh — as soon as possible, details of incident recorded on standard Incident Report Form
- Dependent on severity and at discretion of Project Manager/Diving Superintendent: all further diving operations may be suspended until situation stabilised
- Atlantic Marine Operations Management informed ASAP
- Information extended to Client's work site representative so all parties aware and informed regularly of actions taken

**Post Treatment Procedure:**
- Upon satisfactory completion of treatment for DCI: patient should remain in vicinity of DDC for further **24 hours**
- Patient may be allowed to sleep, although attendant should be assigned to keep watch for signs of recurrence of distress
- Patient should be **awoken every 4 hours** to determine well-being
- On completion of this period: **approved medical practitioner must examine patient** to determine fitness to return to work
- When transporting patient ashore by air: observe rules governing "Flying After Diving"

**Fitness to Return to Diving:**
- **Type I DCI** (musculoskeletal decompression and skin rash):
  - Full recovery: **24 hour lay-off**
  - Recurrence or relapse during or after treatment that required further recompression: **7 day lay-off**
- **Type II DCI** (any serious manifestation, whether or not there is also a limb bend):
  - Full recovery and after examination by 'Approved Doctor': **7 day lay-off or longer period as advised by Doctor**
  - Any residual manifestations (examined by Hyperbaric Medical Specialist): **Unfit for diving**
- **Pulmonary Barotrauma:** following incident of decompression barotrauma, whether or not associated with gas embolism: prolonged lay-off necessary, return to diving subject to assessment by 'Approved Doctor'

### 13.4.0 Chamber Emergencies

**Chamber Fire:**
- In event of smouldering or fire in occupied chamber which cannot be immediately and easily extinguished:
  1. Isolate oxygen from chamber and at quad
  2. Isolate electrical supplies as required
  3. Transfer occupants to adjacent lock
  4. Affected lock to be brought to surface as quickly as possible

**Loss of Chamber Ambient Pressure:**
- Open chamber supplies primary and secondary and attempt to maintain pressure
- If fault cannot be immediately resolved: transfer occupants to adjacent chamber/lock, assess as for omitted decompression etc.

**Oxygen Leak in Chamber:**
- Immediately isolate oxygen at panel valve
- Flush through chamber to maintain oxygen percent at **not greater than 23%**

**Diver Adverse Reaction to Oxygen Breathing:**
- Remove from O2 breathing immediately
- Tender to try and protect diver from injury during any convulsions
- Monitor and assess situation
- Complete decompression on appropriate all-air profile (Air TUP)
- Complete decompression on air by doubling remaining O2 and ascent periods
- Inform medical adviser and monitor affected diver

### 13.5.0 Hyperbaric Abandonment (Air)

**Catastrophic Incident** — Conditions onboard immediately life-threatening, beyond resources of emergency teams to control, communications and essential services have failed, evacuation required immediately:
- **Divers in water when emergency occurs:** recovered to surface at ascent rate not exceeding **25 fsw per minute**. If any decompression penalty: remove diving equipment and commence breathing 100% oxygen from portable oxygen supply unit. Only interruption in breathing oxygen will be for fluid intake. ERP activated and communications established on emergency channels to nearest hyperbaric facility to arrange reception/hyperbaric medical assistance as required. Divers monitored by Diving Supervisor at all times during evacuation.
- **Divers in DDC when emergency occurs:** DDC vented to surface at ascent rate not exceeding **25 fsw per minute**. Once on surface: diver commences breathing 100% oxygen from portable oxygen supply unit. Only interruption for fluid intake. Evacuation then undertaken as per standing instructions for installation. ERP activated and communications established on emergency channels to nearest hyperbaric facility. Divers monitored by Diving Supervisor at all times.

**Major Incident** — Evacuation required but may be undertaken under more controlled circumstances. Emergency teams have control (with or without external assistance). Communications good, essential supply services available:
- Dive abort tables utilised; once on surface diver commences breathing 100% oxygen from portable breathing supply unit. Only interruption for fluid intake. Evacuation then as per standing instructions. Communication established on emergency channels to nearest hyperbaric facility. Divers monitored by Diving Supervisor at all times.

**Potential Major Incident** — Emergency developing which could result in evacuation, emergency teams on standby. All non-essential work ceased, essential supply services prioritised with open communication links:
- Decompression undertaken as per normal procedures with only essential personnel present. Regular communication to facility control room throughout decompression from dive station. Upon completion of decompression: all personnel transfer to muster point or as required with control room advised.

**Summary:**
- If therapeutic treatment being undertaken and catastrophic incident occurs: consideration/evaluation to cease treatment, surface chamber immediately, then adopt ERP
- If therapeutic treatment being undertaken and major incident occurs: ascent to surface from chamber depth should be made on oxygen at not greater than **1.1 metres per minute**

## CHAPTER 14: EMERGENCY RESPONSE PROCEDURE

### Introduction
- Guidance required in event of emergency during operations and work activities
- Written specifically for diving related emergencies, to be used in conjunction with project-specific bridging documents and client information
- Atlantic Marine Management to be informed at earliest opportunity
- Intention: minimize consequences of situation and bring under control as soon as practical
- Initial actions for any emergency similar in most circumstances — follow to avoid confusion

### Classification of Emergencies
Three categories:

**MINOR:**
- Can be contained and controlled by onsite Company Representative
- Do not require assistance from outside work site

**SERIOUS:**
- Require outside support from locally based facilities
- Not major support from Atlantic Marine Management or outside agencies/emergency services
- Company Representative keeps Project Manager updated, who advises senior management of all actions

**MAJOR:**
- Beyond resources of onsite facilities
- Require support from Atlantic Marine Management, additional agencies and emergency services

Each category may be subdivided into ONSHORE and OFFSHORE work groups.

### Onshore Emergency Response Team
- **Managing Director** — Team Leader
- **Operations Manager** — Deputy Team Leader
- **Project Manager**
- **Diving Department Manager**
- **Human Resources Manager**
- **HSE Manager**
- Meet at Company's conference room (or nominated Emergency Response Room)
- In event conference room not available/accessible: meet at muster location outside office
- During response: Team Leader or Deputy Team Leader manages situation and controls all actions
- Types: fire in office premises, bomb threat, flood, earthquake, property damage, loss of computer database/operating system, staff injury, break in, onshore work site emergency

### Offshore Emergency Response Team
- **General Manager** — Team Leader
- **Diving Superintendent** — Team Leader
- **Diving Supervisor**
- **Emergency Operations Center Coordinator**
- **HSE Manager**
- (Client Representative where required)
- Meet at Company's conference room (or nominated Emergency Response Room)
- During response: Team Leader or Deputy Team Leader manages situation and controls all actions
- Types: fire, Client/Contractor Emergency, Diving Emergency, Staff injury, Vessel/property damage, Third Party Emergency, Adverse Weather (Typhoon etc.), Medivac, Fatality

### Communications
- Bridging document completed prior to project commencing with Company office and all onsite contact numbers and email addresses
- Recorded by Project Manager for future use
- Communication check using most suitable means and secondary means of contact shall be made

**General Communications (means):** VHF Radio, Telephone (satellite, GSM hand phone, etc.), Email, Fax

**Media Communications:** Only Team Leader / Deputy Team Leader or nominated Management Representative can release any official information to media.

### Managing the Emergency
- Onsite Company Representative responsible for managing emergency
- Level of emergency indicates level of management response
- Onsite representative to:
  - Assess situation and implement/work with other site representatives to alleviate or control
  - Inform Client and Atlantic Marine Management team
  - Maintain detailed log of events complete with photographic records if possible
  - Contact emergency response agencies
  - Keep Company Emergency Response Team informed at all times
  - In event of serious injury: all plant and equipment maintained in same condition as prior to/during incident (unless leaving it presents continuing hazard). If possible make photographic record prior to dismantling or movement of any equipment.
  - Certain accidents/incidents require reporting to regulatory authorities
  - When employee required to be evacuated from work site: medical company support personnel, medivac co-ordinating centre informed
  - Information accompanying evacuated employee: GPS co-ordinator, helicopter landing zone, presence of helipad, Atlantic Marine personnel looking after casualty, name, contact details, type and extent of injury, vital signs record (see DCI examination sheet), signs and symptoms record, drugs administered and treatment given, next of kin details
  - All medivac cases evacuated to nearest medical facility named in project ERP
  - Once patient stabilised: onward transportation to nominated project Company hospital services at earliest and most suitable time

## CHAPTER 15: RECRUITMENT & CERTIFICATE VERIFICATION

### Recruitment
- Atlantic Marine policy: recruit only Divers and Diving Supervisors holding certificates recognised by IMCA per IMCA D16/16
- Goal: hire and retain "exceptional people"
- Recruitment covers: job analysis, advertising, internal recruitment/promotion, interviews, reference checking, pre-employment health checks, contract preparation
- Process: Identification/Attraction → Selection → Appointment
- Check experience and knowledge of Divers and Supervisors before considering employment (examine past/current log books, contact previous employers/colleagues, pre-employment interviews)
- Internal recruitment used to utilise skills of existing workforce and promote from within
- Equal employment opportunity employer — based on merit (right skills, qualifications, talents), not sex/race/disability/age/sexual orientation or personal biases/favouritism
- All permanent employees on **3 month probationary period**
- At interview stage: asked if employee has any medical/personal considerations which will detriment ability to work at levels expected

### Diver Supervisors
- IMCA Offshore Diving Supervisor Scheme
- Two levels: IMCA Air Diving Supervisor and IMCA Bell Diving Supervisor
- Only issued by IMCA
- To hold IMCA diving supervisor certificate: must sit and pass IMCA examination
- To be eligible: meet minimum entry criteria (appropriate diver qualification + experience), complete and pass IMCA-approved trainee diving supervisor course, complete minimum panel hours under direct supervision offshore as trainee, recommended by company following satisfactory offshore reports
- Diving supervisor and LST certificates issued by AODC (merged to form IMCA in 1995) remain valid (except those listed in IMCA D07/03)
- Comparable certificates: ADAS, DCBC, NDC (Dutch waters only)

### Verification of Qualifications
- Atlantic Marine shall verify validity of certification held by dive team members
- Production of original certificates by dive team members and cross-checking with issuing schools per IMCA guidelines
- **Original certificates presented before recruitment; examined closely for alterations**
- Atlantic Marine shall contact relevant government body/agency/school by phone, email, letter
- Published invalidated certificates consulted
- **Forgery of certificates known to occur in this industry** — this is clear guideline to minimize likelihood of engaging unqualified staff
